Why does MSE go down?

Training updates the weights again and again so predictions move closer to the targets. But backpropagation and gradient descent do different jobs.

Backpropagation finds which way to change; gradient descent takes the step; MSE tells you whether that step helped.

Backpropagation · The backward pass

It does not descend.
It finds the slope.

Suppose the model is simply ŷ = w × x. With input x = 2, target y = 6, and current weight w = 1, the prediction is 2 and the MSE is 16.

Backpropagation starts at MSE and moves backward through the computation graph, multiplying local derivatives with the chain rule to obtain ∂MSE/∂w = −16.

This means a small increase in w should reduce MSE. But at this point, neither w nor MSE has changed yet.

One sample · chain ruleForward pass
The forward pass moves left to right: w = 1 → ŷ = 2 → r = −4 → MSE = 16. It only computes the prediction and error.

With multiple samples, each one contributes a gradient and the results are averaged. Deep networks use the same chain rule—just with a longer computation graph.

Gradient Descent · The update

Once you know the slope,
move the other way.

The gradient points in the direction of the steepest increase in MSE, so the update subtracts it. The learning rate η (eta) controls the step size.

In this example, w = 1, the gradient is −16, and η = 0.1, so the new weight is 1 − 0.1 × (−16) = 2.6. The prediction moves from 2 to 5.2, and MSE falls from 16 to 0.64.

The direct cause of changing MSE is therefore: weights change → predictions change → residuals change → MSE changes.

wnew=w−η×∂MSE/∂w
w: current weightη: step size (learning rate)gradient: computed by backprop

Common misconceptions

MSE does not
fall every time.

After backprop

MSE does not change immediately. Backpropagation only computes gradients. New predictions and a new MSE appear only after the weights are updated.

After gradient descent

The right direction does not guarantee a decrease at every step. A large learning rate can overshoot, and noisy mini-batch gradients can make MSE fluctuate.

When MSE falls

It only means the average squared error on the training data is smaller. This does not automatically mean better performance on unseen data; check validation MSE too.

When MSE stalls

The gradient may be near zero, the learning rate too small, or the model stuck on a plateau. Deep networks can also have local minima and are no longer simple bowls.

The whole story

MSE is the score; backprop is the analyst; gradient descent makes the update.
