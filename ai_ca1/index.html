WHEN SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG

Troubleshooting

Diagnose the cell first. Do not fix an error by typing the expected number over a formula.

My formula appears as text instead of calculating

Check that the entry begins with =. Click the cell, press F2, then Enter. If it still shows text, select the cell, choose Home → Number → General, and re-enter the formula.

I see #NAME?

Check EXP spelling and every sheet name. Use initial!E23, not Initial!E23 with a missing or differently named tab. Also check for a missing ! between sheet and cell.

I see #REF!

A referenced cell or sheet was deleted or a pasted formula points outside the worksheet. Undo the last deletion or paste, then restore the exact reference shown in the manual.

I see #VALUE!

A formula is trying to multiply or add text. Inspect every referenced cell. Retype numbers such as 0.05 without units, labels, spaces, or a leading apostrophe.

The result is close but not identical

First increase decimal places with Home → Increase Decimal. Differences only beyond the displayed precision are normal. If the difference is larger, check the formula rather than rounding intermediate cells.

My total error is not decreasing

Check the four output-layer updates in M23:M26 and the four hidden-layer updates in E23:E26. Then check that the next round links to those updated cells—not back to the original constants.

A round sheet uses the wrong weights

The links form a chain: round1 → initial, round2 → round1, and round3 → round2. Inputs, targets, biases, and learning rate stay constant.

Nothing recalculates after I change a cell

Go to Formulas → Calculation Options → Automatic, then press F9. On a Mac, use Excel → Preferences → Calculation → Automatic and press ⌘ + = if needed.

Final self-check

  1. All eight updated weights exist on every round sheet.
  2. U8 decreases from initial through round3.
  3. Predictions Q17 and Q20 move toward 0.01 and 0.99.
  4. Sheet1 weight differences match the linked round sheets.
  5. No formula has been replaced by a typed answer.
